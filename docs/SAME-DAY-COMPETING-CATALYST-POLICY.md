# Same-day competing-catalyst policy

**Status:** adopted 2026-09-28. Applies to Milestone 2 step 2 going forward, and to any future
depth expansion. Does not retroactively change any Milestone 1 disposition.

## The gap this closes

Milestone 1 excluded Methode Electronics (MEI, `0000065270-26-000005`) because its earnings
release (2026-03-05, 16:30 ET) was preceded 10 minutes earlier by a separate GlobeNewswire release
announcing the sale of its dataMate business (16:20 ET). The finding was recorded in
`reports/m1-mei-competing-catalyst-finding-2026-09-23.json` and left quarantined with an explicit
note: *"this needs a general same-day-competing-catalyst policy, not an ad hoc call on one
candidate"* (`docs/M1-READINESS-ASSESSMENT.md`, September 23 entry). That policy was never written
down — it stayed an open gap through the rest of Milestone 1.

Meanwhile, Milestone 1's own accepted OKTA event (a different quarter from any step-2 OKTA
candidate) has **two** Item 5.02 officer/director-change 8-Ks inside its 20-session reaction
window (`reports/m1-okta-complete-event-2026-09-26.json`), and was still accepted — those are
recorded in `competing_catalysts_inside_window` as a caveat, not treated as disqualifying.

Comparing these two real outcomes is what the policy below is built from: the deciding factor was
never "does a second disclosure exist somewhere in the window," it was the combination of timing,
independent dissemination, and content weight.

## The test

Exclude a candidate for a same-day competing catalyst only when **all three** hold:

1. **Same reaction day** — the competing item was disclosed on the same calendar day as the
   earnings anchor (not merely "somewhere inside the multi-session reaction window," which MEI's
   own comparison to the accepted OKTA event shows is not disqualifying by itself).
2. **Independently disseminated** — the item went out as its own press release, picked up by a
   wire service or the company's own newsroom, separately from the earnings release. An item
   disclosed only inside the 8-K's own text or through an exhibit that is a legal/administrative
   document (a court stipulation, a settlement notice) rather than a press release does not meet
   this bar — the market has no independent, same-day public route to that information the way it
   does for a wire-carried press release.
3. **Fresh, independently material content** — the substance is new as of that day and weighty
   enough to plausibly move price on its own (a business divestiture, a clinical trial readout,
   an unplanned executive departure "for cause"). A long-telegraphed, orderly succession, or a
   procedural update on litigation that was already settled and preliminarily court-approved weeks
   earlier, does not meet this bar even if it happens to be mentioned the same day.

If any one of the three fails, the candidate is not excluded on this basis — it may still carry a
caveat (recorded, not hidden) for whoever later reads the accepted-event evidence.

This is a human-judgment aid, not a mechanical classifier: satisfying condition 3 still requires
actually reading the disclosure's text, the way every application below did.
